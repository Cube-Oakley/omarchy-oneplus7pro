# Speaker output: mic loopback measurements — September 22, 2026

Follows the [microphone work](microphone-20260922.md). Result: **the upper amplifier
and earpiece work; the bottom loudspeaker is silent although its amplifier is
configured and running exactly like the working one.** That is why YouTube and
other media were inaudible.

## Method

`scripts/phone-speaker-loopback.py` (installed as
`/root/audio-bringup/speaker-loopback.py`) suspends the PipeWire sink and source,
records one AMIC input while playing a faded 1.5 s tone straight to the
MultiMedia1 PCM (bypassing the route cap, refused above -12 dBFS), and
compares the tone-frequency component (Hann-windowed Goertzel) before and during
the tone. AMIC5 has no microphone behind it and is the electrical-coupling
control. Left is the upper amplifier (0x34, TDM slot 0), right the lower one
(0x35, slot 1). `LOOPBACK_TIMELINE=1` prints every 100 ms window.

heard only the low beeps, from the **top earpiece**, faint (about a quarter of
normal call volume with the phone at the ear), and nothing from the bottom.

## What was ruled out

- **Data line:** stock `sm8150-oem.dtsi` overrides quaternary MI2S to RX on SD1
  (`qcom,msm-mi2s-rx-lines = <2>`, a bitmask) and TX on SD0. Our
  `qcom,sd-lines = <1>` (a line number) is the same SD1. Stock uses only
  `QUAT_MI2S_RX` for speakers, never TDM.
- **Slot data:** with register 0x26 of 0x35 temporarily set to slot 0, which
  carries audio the upper amplifier plays, the bottom mic level was unchanged
  (-57.9 vs -57.8 dBFS, leakage from the earpiece). Restored to `0x1001` and
  read back over I2C (`out/audio-test/20260922/slot-swap-test.log`).
- **Amplifier configuration:** all 46 common fields match the related-device
  reference, whose bottom speaker worked with the same status values. Both
  amplifiers' 43 readable registers are identical idle and playing except the
  per-slot fields 0x26/0x27/0x70 (which match the stock container for each
  chip), sensor readings and the hidden key. The stock `speaker` profile's
  five fields are already applied. `calibrate.cal` carries ADSP protection
  messages, not amplifier settings.
- **Board wiring:** stock describes only a reset GPIO per amplifier (37 for
  0x34, 100 for 0x35); no enable GPIO or separate supply. The lower amplifier
  answers I2C and reports running, so it is not held in reset; both measure the
  same VDDP.

## Most likely cause

A hardware fault between the lower amplifier and the bottom speaker, most
likely an open connection: the TFA9874 cannot report an open load without the
ADSP protection algorithm. The phone was opened to work on the battery and
charge port and later reassembled. The user recalls loud but "weird" audio in
Havoc OS a few weeks ago; that is consistent with media playing through the
earpiece alone in its speaker profile. Not yet confirmed: reseating the bottom
speaker, or a stock-software check in Lineage on slot A.

## Resolved: the bottom board was not seated

The user opened the phone again. The bottom board sat crooked and the screw next
to the speaker contacts was missing; they reseated it (and refitted the missing
NFC antenna piece). On the next boot the same loopback measured the bottom
speaker at **-42.2 dBFS (+39.6 dB)** for a -24 dBFS right-channel tone, with the
AMIC5 control at +3.3 dB, and the user heard the high beeps from the bottom.
Log: `out/audio-test/20260922/speaker-after-reseat.log`. Both amplifiers and both
speakers now work. (That tone was S24_LE, so really -72 dBFS.)

## True levels: the 24-bit path was 48 dB low

With the PipeWire sinks suspended, S16 at -60 dBFS and S24_LE at -12 dBFS gave
the same mic level (-40.4 and -40.6 dBFS). The hardware PCM accepts only S16_LE
and S24_LE, and the ADSP plays S24_LE samples 8 bits (48 dB) low, as if they
were MSB-aligned 32-bit words. The loopback script, the sustained tests and the
ALSA cap all used S24_LE, so the "-36 dB" cap was really about -84 dB. That is
why media was inaudible. The script and the cap now use S16_LE.

True levels (S16 straight to MultiMedia1, 1 kHz unless noted; distortion is
H2 and H3 as a percentage of the fundamental):

| Bottom | -22 | AMIC4 (0:64) | 3.2% |

## Output cap and processing sink

`alsa-quiet.conf` converts to S16_LE and plays a mono mix, capped where a 1 kHz
tone reaches about 5% distortion: bottom speaker -18 dBFS, earpiece -42 dBFS.
The earpiece is then about 17 dB quieter, so both play (L+R)/2 and a
hard-panned source is not lost. Different tones on the two inputs arrived equal
at the mic (within 0.7 dB) with either assignment, so the table is not reversed;
a reversed table would have put the bottom speaker's level on the earpiece.

Worst case: 30 s of 400 Hz high-passed noise at -9 dBFS RMS, clipped at full
scale (a hot master at 100% volume), through the cap. The bottom mic level
drifted -0.3 dB (`out/audio-test/20260922/sustained-cap-test.log`; that run did
not capture amplifier temperatures). Longer sessions at full volume are
untested.

`20-guacamole-output.conf` makes the default sink a processing sink: a 4th-order
400 Hz high-pass, the swh-plugins SC4 RMS compressor (threshold -24 dB, ratio 3,
knee 6 dB, makeup +10 dB) and the fast look-ahead limiter at -1 dBFS.
`swh-plugins` was installed on the phone with pacman. Recorded from the hardware
sink's monitor through the normal path: -40 dBFS in gives -30 out, -1 dBFS gives
-4.4, and clipped noise peaks at exactly -1.0 dBFS
(`out/audio-test/20260922/dsp-check-1.log`). The 400 Hz filter is not what
makes the speaker sound thin: the speaker is 13 dB down at 500 Hz and 29 dB down
at 300 Hz, while the filter costs 3 dB at 400 Hz and under 1 dB at 500 Hz.
Stock software instead runs NXP speaker protection on the ADSP and Dolby tuning.

Virtual bass, built from filter-chain builtins: the band below 250 Hz becomes a
square wave that follows the bass envelope, and its 450–1500 Hz harmonics are
mixed back. On an 80 Hz tone the fundamental dropped 46 dB and harmonics came out
at -15 to -26 dBFS. On music the user heard heavy distortion, because the square
wave turns chords and overlapping notes into intermodulation. It is off
(mixer gain 0) until a gentler generator replaces it.

## PipeWire playback: the batch buffer

Through the volume-group loopbacks the hardware PCM sat in SETUP or XRUN with
the hardware sink's error count climbing, so the default path was silent. The
ADSP moves the pointer a period at a time, so PipeWire treats the PCM as batch:
it halves the period to 512 frames and keeps an extra period queued. A 3-period
buffer (1536 frames) cannot hold that fill. 2048 frames of headroom let a fresh
start run, but music ticked audibly and reopening the sink mid-stream fell back
into an XRUN loop. With 8 periods (4096 frames, the hardware maximum) and 1024
frames of headroom, playback runs at quanta 512 and 2048 and through a
mid-stream reopen with no errors, and the ticking stopped. The user reports
YouTube clean and louder than before, though not as loud as it could be.

## Notes for later

- Loudness: a stronger leveler, a multiband compressor, or a -15 dBFS bottom
  limit (7–8% distortion) after a longer thermal test.
- Rework the virtual bass with a gentler harmonic generator.
- Calls need their own earpiece level check; the -42 dBFS media limit is not a
  call volume.
- The mic loopback remains the objective loudness and regression reference.
- Mic default gain is ADC4 12 / DEC0 88.
