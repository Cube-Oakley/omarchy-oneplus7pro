# Camera bring-up — September 22–23, 2026

Goal: the first frame from one rear camera, the main Sony IMX586. This note
records the power groundwork, the runtime stack and the main-camera slot.
Everything loads at runtime from modules and DT overlays; kernel #190 adds only
what cannot be added at runtime.

## Research

Three host-only reviews (no hardware touched):

- **Stock wiring.** Guacamole's stock camera tree (LineageOS 23.2 DTBO entry 00,
  `sm8150-oem-camera-t0.dtsi`) is **identical to the OnePlus 7T Pro's** (19801)
  once phandles are resolved: CCI, pinctrl, CSIPHY, camcc/GDSC, flash, pop-up
  motor and Hall nodes. The vendor image ships exactly four sensor libraries.

  | Slot | Sensor | CCI, address | PHY | VDIG (PM8009) | Enables |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Rear main | IMX586 | CCI0 master 1, 0x1a | CSIPHY1, 3-trio C-PHY | LDO1 1.104 V | GPIO11, GPIO29, PM8150L GPIO1 |
  | Telephoto | S5K3M5 | CCI0 master 0, 0x10 | CSIPHY0, 4 lanes | LDO2 | GPIO148 |
  | Front pop-up | IMX471 | CCI1 master 0, 0x10 | CSIPHY2, 4 lanes | LDO3 1.056 V | PM8150L GPIO12 |
  | Ultrawide | IMX481 | CCI1 master 1, 0x1a | CSIPHY3, 4 lanes | LDO4 1.056 V | PM8150L GPIO2 |

  All share VIO (PM8150L LDO1, 1.8 V), VANA from BOB (3.3 V) behind a switch,
  MCLK 19.2 MHz and active-low resets. Stock power order: VANA, VDIG, VIO,
  MCLK, reset; the IMX586 adds its two extra enables after VANA and VDIG.
- **The 7T Pro port** (`.work/hotdog-reference`, r181) captures from all four
  sensors through CAMSS raw capture and libcamera's software ISP. Its own
  SM8150 CAMSS backend, sensor drivers and DT carry over. Its main hazard: VDIG
  before VANA tripped the PMIC's undervoltage lockout, an instant power cut
  with no logs, and repeated ones left the phone in crashdump mode.
- **Our 6.17 tree** builds CAMSS, CCI and the media core as modules but has no
  SM8150 CAMSS entry, no CCI/CAMSS nodes and no IMX586 driver, and
  `CONFIG_SM_CAMCC_8150` is unset.

## Power: CAMCC without releasing the modem

Until rpmhpd's `sync_state` runs, every RPMh power domain is clamped to its top
corner; CAMCC is the last unsatisfied consumer. Loading CAMCC earlier released
the clamp and the modem's MSS hold, and the next suspend failed
([deeper suspend](deeper-suspend-20260917.md),
[MSS handoff](mss-handoff-test-20260917.md)).

`guacamole_rpmhpd_hold.ko` (`devices/oneplus7pro/kernel/power/rpmhpd_hold.c`,
`scripts/build_rpmhpd_hold.sh`) consumes the domains voted before sync (MSS,
MX, CX, MMCX) and requests a level above every RPMh level, which rpmhpd maps to
each domain's top corner. Loaded before `camcc-sm8150.ko`, from cmd-db-named
vote-cache snapshots (`rpmh_votes.ko`):

```
before: synced=0 xo=3/3 mmcx=6/6 mx=7/7 cx=7/7 mss=-1/9
after:  synced=1 xo=3/3 mmcx=6/6 mx=7/7 cx=7/7 mss=-1/9
```

CAMCC bound and sync ran with no rail moving; the modem, ADSP, Wi-Fi, audio and
Bluetooth stayed healthy. The first trial also held EBI, LMX, LCX and GFX,
which carry no AP vote before sync and only gained one (the GPU rail pinned at
its top corner until reboot); the module now holds the four voted domains.
Suspend with CAMCC and the hold loaded is untested; the phone stays plugged in
during camera work. Logs: `out/camera/logs/power/`.

A likely proper fix is the 7T Pro's: keep the modem's proxy CX/MSS votes until
it stops (their `0156`), which also fixed their first-suspend modem watchdog.

## Runtime stack

- `scripts/build_camera_modules.sh`: mc, videodev, v4l2-async, v4l2-fwnode,
  videobuf2 (common, memops, v4l2, dma-sg), `qcom-camss` with the 7T Pro's
  SM8150 patches (`devices/oneplus7pro/kernel/camera/camss/`), `i2c-qcom-cci`,
  and the 7T Pro's `imx586.c`.
- `scripts/build_camera_overlay.sh`: `guacamole-camera.dts` (CCI0, CAMSS, PM8009
  LDO1/3/4, the IMX586 slot) and `guacamole_camera.ko`, which refuses unless
  the hold and CAMCC are bound.
- `scripts/phone-camera-test.sh power|bus|sensor`, once per boot, nothing
  unloads.

Two ordering lessons from the first run:

- **Load the CCI driver after the overlay.** With it loaded, CCI probed in the
  middle of the overlay apply, the I2C core then took its `i2c-bus@N` nodes for
  client devices, and the notifier error rolled the whole overlay back.
- **PM8009 LDO1/3/4** are new children of an already bound regulator device;
  the bus stage unbinds and rebinds it (no users). LDO1 needs
  `regulator-allow-set-load` for the driver's 1.1 A VDIG vote.

Bus stage result (no sensor power): CCI0 bound with two masters, the IMX586
client at `4-001a`, CAMSS bound with `/dev/media0` and eight video nodes, PM8009
LDO1 1.104 V and LDO3/4 1.056 V registered.

## PM8150L and kernel #190

The IMX586 probe stayed deferred with no message: its pinctrl includes
PM8150L GPIO1 ("CAM_PVDD"), and PM8150L was disabled. The native3 power
overlay (`guacamole-power-support.dts`) starts the SPMI bus and enabled only
the charger PMIC; SPMI devices are created only when the bus starts, so this
cannot be fixed from the camera overlay. The overlay now enables PM8150L with
only its GPIO block (ADC, thermal alarm and PON stay off).

That overlay lives in `power_support.ko` inside the built-in initramfs, so it
needs a new kernel image. **Kernel #190** (`scripts/build_kernel190.sh`) is #189
plus that module and the `hsuart0` Bluetooth alias in the boot DTB. Same
source, `.config` and release string. Only that one of 65 initramfs entries
changed (the archive is rewritten entry by entry); the running module was
first matched byte for byte to the overlay's RTC variant. Flash:
`scripts/flash_kernel190.sh test`; rollback to #189: `… rollback`.

## Result on #190: the main camera streams

#190 booted (boot `c3032c07-2c7c-46f7-848d-7ff164f960d6`) with the alias and
the PM8150L GPIO controller bound; modem, ADSP, Wi-Fi and audio as before. The
three stages then ran in order:

- **power:** sync ran with MMCX 6/6, MX and CX 7/7 and MSS 9 unchanged; EBI,
  LMX, LCX and GFX show a 0 vote (no AP vote, as before).
- **bus:** as above.
- **sensor:** the IMX586 powered up in the stock order, answered with chip ID
  0x0586 and bound; CAMSS completed its graph (`imx586 4-001a` on
  `/dev/v4l-subdev14`, an enabled immutable link to `msm_csiphy1`,
  SRGGB10 4000x3000 from an 8000x6000 array) and runtime-suspended the sensor.

Raw capture, with CSIPHY1 → CSID0 → VFE0 RDI0 linked and every pad at
`SRGGB10_1X10/4000x3000`:

```
v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 --set-fmt-video=width=4000,height=3000,pixelformat=pRAA
v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 --stream-mmap --stream-count=4 --stream-to=/tmp/imx586.raw
imx586 4-001a: streaming 4000x3000 RAW10 over three-trio C-PHY
```

Four frames of 15,024,000 bytes (5008-byte stride) with no kernel errors. With
the phone face down the frame was pure black level (64 ± 0.5): real sensor
data, unlike the 7T Pro's first untouched buffers. Propped up, in a dim room at
the driver defaults (exposure 3018 lines, the maximum; analogue gain code 112),
the channels rose to means of 65.5–68.3 with structure. A 2x2-binned, gray-world
preview shows the room, blurred because nothing drives the lens yet. Frames and
previews stay in ignored `out/camera/frames/` (they picture the user's room).
Logs: `out/camera/logs/`.

## libcamera: processed frames at 30 fps

libcamera 0.7.2 (Arch ARM `libcamera`, `libcamera-tools`, `libcamera-ipa`)
finds the camera through the simple pipeline with the software ISP:

```
1: Internal back camera (/base/soc@0/cci@ac4a000/i2c-bus@1/camera@1a)
cam -c1 --capture=20 -s width=1280,height=960,pixelformat=ABGR8888 --file=/tmp/cam/frame-#.bin
... (29.51 fps) cam0-stream0 seq: 000017 bytesused: 4915200
```

The ISP offers ABGR8888 from 160x120 up to 3992x3000. It warns that there is
no IMX586 sensor helper, static properties or tuning file, so it runs
`uncalibrated.yaml`: frames come out dark with a colour cast. The 7T Pro's
libcamera patches add the IMX586 helper (gain `1024/(1024-code)`, black level
64); that is the next quality step.

## Focus

Stock fits either actuator: LC898217XC on Samsung Electro-Mechanics modules,
AK7374 on O-Film ones. `guacamole-camera-lens-probe.dts` powers the 3.3 V VAF
rail (BOB through the GPIO25 switch) and offers both; the LC898217XC driver
identifies its chip, and it answered at 0x72:

```
lc898217xc 4-0072: LC898217XC actuator ready
```

(The AK7374 client also bound, but the upstream `ak7375` driver never reads
the chip at probe, so that proves nothing.) The camera overlay now carries the
VAF regulator, the lens at 0x72 and the sensor's `lens-focus` link; the bus
stage loads `lc898217xc.ko` first, because the sensor completes only once its
lens has bound. The lens appears as `/dev/v4l-subdev15` (function Lens).
Drivers: `scripts/build_camera_lens.sh` (the 7T Pro's `lc898217xc.c`,
byte-identical to their final blob, and their AK7374 patch for `ak7375.c`).

`scripts/phone-camera-focus-sweep.py` steps focus 0–400 and scores a centre
Laplacian. Two lessons: the driver ignores focus writes while the actuator is
runtime-suspended, and it drops back to rest once suspended, so the script
holds the lens node open; and at the driver's default gain (code 112, about
1.1x) a dim room is too noisy to score, so the sweep ran at code 800 (about
4.6x). Result, 35% above the out-of-focus floor:

```
focus 200: sharpness 35.5   focus 250: 45.7   focus 300: 43.3   focus 350: 34.4
```

At 250 the full-resolution centre resolves the wall's plaster texture and a
handwritten label is legible. With the phone on its side, charge port on the
right, the raw frame is upside down; apps get the orientation from libcamera's
Rotation property (the DT's `rotation = <90>` is for portrait).

## libcamera with the IMX586 helper and autofocus (September 23)

The 7T Pro bring-up patches exactly our libcamera, 0.7.2: an IMX586 sensor
helper (gain `1024/(1024-code)`, black level 4096 on the 16-bit scale, that
is 64 at 10 bits), static properties (0.8 µm cells, two-frame control
delays), contrast autofocus for the software ISP, and faster, steadier 3A.
Their tuning file, `imx586.yaml`, enables black level, white balance,
adjust, exposure and autofocus. All ten apply cleanly to the 0.7.2 tarball
(checksum as their build pins it). They live in
`devices/oneplus7pro/camera/libcamera/`, with one of ours:

- `0011` lets the dma-buf allocator retry from the system heap. The software
  ISP accepts CMA, system and udmabuf buffers but used only the first
  provider it could open, the 32 MiB CMA heap, so a 3992x3000 ABGR8888 frame
  (48 MB) failed with `dma-heap allocation failure`. The GPU and display sit
  behind IOMMUs, so non-contiguous buffers are fine.

`scripts/build_libcamera_on_phone.sh` verifies the tarball, applies the
patches and builds on the phone (meson, ninja, python-jinja, python-yaml and
python-ply installed with pacman) into `/opt/libcamera-guacamole`, leaving the
system package alone. Only the simple pipeline, its IPA and `cam` are built;
the software ISP runs on the GPU (EGL, GLES 3.2), its default when EGL is
available.

Results, phone propped on its side facing plants and a statue under a lamp:

```
1280x960  ABGR8888: 30.0 fps; IPASoftAf: Focus range 0-400, steps 100/20
                    Autofocus completed at 400 after about 20 frames
3992x3000 ABGR8888: 15 fps processed (the sensor still delivers 30)
```

At 1:1 the full frame resolves the statue's cast texture and paint flecks.
The stock package gives the same exposure and tone at 1280x960, softer
without autofocus. Both look flat and slightly hazy (shadows at 33-36/255);
the tuning has no contrast curve or colour matrix. Contrast and Saturation
are runtime controls an app can set.

### A crash during a build

The first capture on kernel #191 ran while the libcamera build kept all eight
cores at full clock. The phone froze into Qualcomm crash-dump mode
(`05c6:900e`) and needed a three-button reset. Sahara reads timed out as
before, and pstore is off: the DT's ramoops region is disabled because an
early mapping of it hung during bring-up, although stock uses the same
address. With the phone otherwise idle, the same stack streamed at 30 fps and
at full resolution without trouble. The difference could be load, heat
(cores reached 84 °C) or #191's CPU scaling, since every earlier capture ran
on #190. The crash also zero-filled 15 object files written just before it;
the build recovered once they were removed.

After that reset the USB gadget stayed `not attached` (the charger saw VBUS)
while Wi-Fi came up. The port-23 recovery shell answers on every interface,
so a clean reboot through it restored USB without buttons.

### System package, PipeWire and Snapshot

`devices/oneplus7pro/camera/libcamera/PKGBUILD` packages the same patches as
`libcamera-guacamole`, which provides and replaces Arch's `libcamera`,
`libcamera-ipa` and `libcamera-tools` (including the `libcamera.so=0.7-64`
provides that `pipewire-libcamera` needs). `scripts/phone-libcamera-package.sh`
builds it on the phone as `nobody` under `/var/tmp` and can install it. It keeps
binaries unstripped, because stripping after install breaks the IPA module
signature; `IPAManager` reports the signature valid, so the IPA runs in
process. Rollback is the cached 0.7.2-4 packages.

With `pipewire-libcamera` installed, WirePlumber's V4L2 monitor listed each raw
CAMSS video node as its own "Qualcomm Camera Subsystem" camera. The monitor
profile (`devices/oneplus7pro/audio/20-guacamole-monitor.conf`, installed by
hand into `~/.config/wireplumber/wireplumber.conf.d/`) now disables
`monitor.v4l2` and enables `monitor.libcamera`, which leaves one "Built-in
Back Camera". GNOME Snapshot 50 with `xdg-desktop-portal` and its GTK backend
asks for camera access once, then shows the live preview (user-confirmed).
Its first photo failed with "Could not find XDG_PICTURES_DIR";
`~/.config/user-dirs.dirs` now names `~/Pictures` and `~/Videos`.

### Autofocus at preview sizes: statistics from one corner

Snapshot streams 1920x1080, and there autofocus scanned a nearly flat
sharpness curve (220k-233k across the lens range) and landed anywhere. At
1280x960 it was flat too; at 3992x3000 the same scene peaked fivefold at 400,
and the raw sweep (`phone-camera-focus-sweep.py`, which bypasses libcamera)
agreed: 340-370 up to 300, 810 at 400. The lens moves; the statistics were
wrong. The GPU debayer scales the whole input to the output size, but set the
statistics window to an output-sized rectangle at the origin, which suits
only the CPU debayer's centre crop. Below full resolution, AGC, AWB and
autofocus measured only the top-left corner of the sensor. Our `0012` gathers
them over the visible part of the input.

`0013` fixes the scan timing. Statistics come every frame and up to four
requests are queued, but the scan skipped one sample after each lens move, so
the next frames were still exposed at the old position: after a run that left
the lens at 400, the first coarse sample at 0 read 1.65M, above every real
position, and the scan settled at 100. It now waits four samples after a step
and six after a jump. Three runs in a row (1280x960 twice, then 1920x1080)
gave the same curve, about 370k at 0 rising to 1.6-1.7M at 400, and all landed
at 400. In Snapshot the user's photos came out in focus and saved; quality is
still modest, and switching between the photo viewer and the preview lags.

### The focus actuator stays powered

WirePlumber's libcamera keeps the lens subdevice open for as long as it runs,
and the LC898217XC driver powered the actuator on open, so the voice coil held
the lens away from rest with the camera idle: the sensor, CAMSS and CCI were
runtime-suspended, the lens active. The lens was also the one camera part on
during the second crash below. Now `imx586` adds a stateless runtime PM device
link to the device named by its `lens-focus` phandle
(`i2c:4-0072--i2c:4-001a`), and the lens driver no longer powers up on open.
Idle, both are suspended; streaming, both are active and autofocus still
lands at 400; three seconds after the stream stops, both are suspended again.
A focus position set while the lens is off is kept and applied at power-up.

### Tuning: a borrowed colour matrix and contrast

The user's Snapshot photos were flat, with grey blacks, a slight green cast and
blown highlights around a lamp. Four captures of one scene at 1920x1080 through
`LIBCAMERA_SIMPLE_TUNING_FILE` and a `cam --script` compared: the base tuning;
the IMX519 colour matrices from libcamera's Raspberry Pi data (BSD-2-Clause, a
similar-era Sony stacked sensor); those plus contrast 1.2 and saturation 1.1;
and the IMX708 matrices with the same adjustments. The IMX519 table with the
adjustments looked most natural (the IMX708's pushed greens toward cyan). The
software ISP applies saturation only when a colour matrix is enabled, and its
Adjust algorithm ignored tuning data, so `0014` reads `contrast` and
`saturation` defaults from the tuning file for apps that set neither, Snapshot
included. `imx586.yaml` now carries the IMX519 table and those defaults; a
capture without a script matched the scripted one. The user found the photos
less washed out. An IMX586 calibration would replace the borrowed table.

### Snapshot caps photos at 1080p

Snapshot's photos were 1920x1080. Its camera library, aperture, picks the
largest stream up to 1080 lines, preferring 16:9, and takes photos from that
same stream, so libcamera tuning cannot give it the 12 MP sensor mode. It also
has no tap-to-focus: autofocus always meters the centre, so on a laptop it
focused the keyboard, not the screen.

KDE's Plasma Camera (26.08, `plasma-camera`, installed with `pipewire-jack`
so FFmpeg's JACK dependency does not pull in `jack2`) uses libcamera directly.
It found the camera, rotated the preview correctly, and offers photos (five
full-resolution frames), video through Qt Multimedia and an exposure control.
But its viewfinder runs at libcamera's default 3992x3000 and copies every frame
into a `QImage` on the CPU: 1.34 cores busy and 72 °C with the preview
open, back to 48 °C 20 s after closing it. It has no tap-to-focus either.

## Omarchy Camera

Neither Snapshot (1080p photos, no tap to focus) nor Plasma Camera (full
resolution preview copied on the CPU) fits, so the user chose our own app,
built to carry to other phones. The engine, `overlay/mobile/camera`, is a
QML module (`OmarchyCamera`) that uses libcamera's public API only; board
specifics stay in the kernel and the sensor tuning file. It builds with
CMake (`install.sh` does it when cmake, ninja and libcamera are present) and
needs `QT_NO_KEYWORDS`, since libcamera's `Signal` has a member named `emit`.

- `CameraSession` runs every libcamera call except `queueRequest()` on a
  worker thread. The preview is a 1440x1080 4:3 stream with continuous
  autofocus. A tap sets `AfWindows` (a fifth of the sensor area around the
  point) and `AfMetering` to windows; a long press returns to the centre.
- `Viewfinder` imports each finished dma-buf as an EGL image bound to a GL
  texture, one per buffer, so frames never pass through the CPU (a mapped
  copy is the fallback). A frame is given back two frames after it left the
  screen. With the preview open the app used 0.58 cores and the phone sat at
  56 °C; Plasma Camera used 1.34 cores and reached 72 °C.
- A still stops the preview, configures the `StillCapture` role at
  3992x3000, holds the lens where the preview focused, waits for the lens and
  for exposure, gain and colour gains to repeat, copies one frame and
  restarts the preview: about 0.6 s. `StillWriter` saves an upright JPEG
  (quality 92) with the phone's make and model (device tree or DMI), date,
  exposure and ISO. `properties::Rotation` is counter-clockwise, so the app
  turns frames clockwise by the rest of a full turn, as Plasma Camera does.
- The app (`overlay/mobile/apps/camera`, permission `camera`) runs the
  camera only while its window is in front, shows the latest photo and a
  swipeable gallery over the running preview, and answers `qs ipc call
  camera capture|focus X Y|state` for tests.

The first stills were green (mean RGB 104/138/74) and soft, because the
software ISP restarted exposure, white balance and autofocus on every
configuration, and start controls never reached it. `0015` keeps exposure
and white balance across a configuration of the same sensor mode (the same
still: 87/92/74), `0017` keeps the focus and resumes watching instead of
scanning after a still, and `0016` fits the autofocus peak between fine
samples, which the user saw settle slightly off. The app repeats its focus
controls on the first request of each stream. A still of the propped-up
scene then came out sharp at 1:1 with natural colour, at ISO 3200 and 1/40 s
in a dim room, so noise is visible.

The user's reference, a GrapheneOS Pixel 9 Pro photo of the same scene, keeps
the salt lamp's texture and lifts the shadows cleanly; ours blows the lamp
out. That needs exposure that protects highlights and a multi-frame merge.

### Crashes during builds

A second crash into `05c6:900e` came during the package rebuild, with the
camera stack loaded and PipeWire's libcamera plugin running but nothing
streaming (the user had closed Snapshot seven minutes earlier; PipeWire logged
nothing). Every build without the stack loaded has finished, including one
with it loaded before PipeWire used it. So the pattern is heavy CPU load with
the camera stack loaded, and at least once with the camera untouched. The
kernel has no lockup detectors, netconsole or working pstore, so a hard hang is
silent until the watchdog fires. The package script now refuses to build with
the stack loaded and uses four jobs at lower priority.

USB stayed `not attached` after both three-button resets out of crash-dump
mode, and after the September 22 one, while every clean reboot (through the
port-23 shell over Wi-Fi) brought it back. The USB kernel messages of a failed
and a working boot are identical, including the PM8150B Type-C overlay at
142 s, so the difference is likely hardware state left by a full PMIC reset.

## Next

- Dynamic range: exposure compensation in the software ISP, highlight-aware
  metering and a tone curve, then a burst merge (noise and highlights, as
  HDR+ does). Video, landscape orientation and a faster still path.
- Quality: an IMX586 colour calibration, lens shading and noise reduction,
  then multi-frame capture (HDR, night) and the other three cameras.
- Catch the crash under load: a kernel with the soft and hard lockup detectors
  and a working pstore (ramoops at the stock address hung once in early
  bring-up), then a CPU stress test with the camera stack loaded.
- USB often stays `not attached` after a crash reset (twice so far, September
  22 and 23), though a clean reboot restores it. Track it down with the crash.
- The other three sensors, one slot at a time, then the pop-up motor with the
  user present.
- Suspend with the camera stack loaded is untested; so is CAMSS recovery after a
  failed stream (the 7T Pro needed a reboot).
